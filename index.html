About

My research begins with a simple question: when an AI system proposes code, a mathematical construction, or a research result, what evidence should make us trust it?

I build evaluation environments and independent checks for reasoning-heavy tasks. In one line of work, I develop runners, certificate-based verifiers, and reproducible evaluation infrastructure for language models finding logical errors in combinatorial reductions. In another, I use graph search and tensor-based certification to study optimization gadgets for Rydberg-atom arrays.

I am particularly interested in software agents, research agents, benchmark reliability, and formal methods. I enjoy projects where theoretical structure and careful engineering reinforce one another.

AutoResearch for Reduction-Rule Discovery

2026–present

Trustworthy research agents · HKUST(GZ)

I work on the problem-finding component of an automated research workflow for discovering combinatorial reduction rules. The system makes candidate mappings explicit through polynomial-time contracts, preserves counterexamples across propose–test–diagnose rounds, and separates generation from independent verification. It has produced 33 candidate rules so far; the project remains in progress and is not yet fully public.

LLM Combinatorial Reasoning Benchmark

2025–2026

Prof. Jinguo Liu's group · HKUST(GZ)

I developed early SWE-style and mini-SWE-agent runners for evaluating language models on subtle logical bugs in problem reductions. My work included a common agent interface, concurrent execution, trajectory archiving, certificate-based checks through the pred verifier, and cost-normalized metrics. Early results helped motivate the shift from bug-finding alone toward automated research.

Unit-Weight Gadgets for Rydberg-Atom Arrays

2025–present

Quantum Computing & Graph Optimization Lab · HKUST(GZ)

I study graph gadgets that encode maximum independent set under the geometric constraints of triangular Rydberg lattices. After direct reinforcement-learning and search approaches repeatedly failed, our work moved toward constructive graph decomposition and exhaustive certification. I contributed reduced-α tensor checks, lattice-animal enumeration, port validation, and a tensor-preserving vertex-split construction. A manuscript is in preparation.
